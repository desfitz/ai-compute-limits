## 2026-09-29 — Viraj Mehta (commodities investor, London), on v24
1. **Economic vs accounting depreciation.** Marginal cost of inference falls with each chip generation; the *economic* depreciation of an installed accelerator is 80-90%/yr at current rates versus 20-30%/yr accounting. Implication: the paper's straight-line 3.5-5 year silicon life is generous; a declining-balance schedule tied to cost-per-token improvement would front-load the carry into 2027-28 and is the mechanism ASC 360 fair-value write-downs would follow. -> Candidate secondary variable `econ_depreciation_rate` (Triang 0.5/0.7/0.9); show carry under both schedules.
2. **Utilization.** Inference FLOP/throughput utilization is modeled at 40-50% (latency, peak buffer). Batching, quantization and open-source proliferation could double useful utilization for the heaviest workloads. Implication: existing stock can serve ~2x the tokens; if token prices fall in step (Jevons), dollar receipts per chip do not rise. -> Register entry `inference_utilization` (0.40/0.45/0.50 today; secondary: doubling case).
3. **Commodity view.** Prices converge to marginal cash cost; uneconomic fixed assets are written off or replaced by lighter structures. The metric to monitor is the marginal cash cost of inference ($ per million tokens) versus realized price. -> New subsection "The commodity floor": receipts if tokens priced at marginal cash cost (~power + opex) versus carry; register entries for token price index and marginal cash cost.
4. **Market context.** Neoclouds are the most popular short among hedge funds; Jim Chanos is publicly vocal on the uneconomic nature of the projects. -> Add to observables/market-context with appropriate hedging; reference for the neo-cloud row.
Status: incorporated in v25 (29 Sept 2026, pre-earnings release), Section "Economic Depreciation, Utilization, and the Commodity Floor"; four register entries added; acknowledged as first reviewer.
